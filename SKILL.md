# Short Satirical Animation Scriptwriter

Build a tiny story, not a compressed explainer. Preserve the subject's recognizable voice while making the comedy readable through performance and action.

## Companion Skills

Load these when available:

- `animated-video-scriptwriter` for timed scene construction.
- `comedy-writing` for setup, misdirection, escalation, tags, and punch-word placement.
- `dialogue-polisher` for distinct character voice and non-generic phrasing.
- `anti-ai-tics` for the final language gate.

## Workflow

1. Lock the brief: audience, platform, duration, language, aspect ratio, named people, permissions, and non-negotiable facts.
2. Label every claim as `confirmed`, `roadmap`, or `satirical exaggeration`. Do not present the last two as current fact.
3. Choose one dominant comic engine: escalation, literalization, incongruity, status reversal, callback, or benign violation. Add a second only if it strengthens the same ending.
4. Define the change across a three-part micro-arc:
   - Beginning, 0-20%: clear question, desire, or normal premise.
   - Middle, 20-75%: concrete mechanism followed by escalating consequence.
   - End, 75-100%: reversal or button that reinterprets the premise.
5. Write a voice card for each speaker: sentence length, vocabulary, verbal habits, pace, confidence, and what the character would never say.
6. Draft spoken dialogue before visual directions. Keep one thought per sentence and put the reveal word as late as natural.
7. Add visual beats that perform the joke. Do not illustrate every noun or replace character reactions with explanatory screens.
8. Add delivery notes only where performance changes meaning: pause, interruption, reflection, excitement, deadpan, or reaction.
9. Run `scripts/check_timing.py` on each speaker's dialogue. Budget pauses separately.
10. Apply the gates below and revise until all pass.

## Short-Form Rules

- Enter at the latest possible moment and leave immediately after the button.
- Use setup plus escalation plus button. A list of funny statements is not a story.
- Preserve one visual world, character design system, and camera grammar throughout.
- Let reactions carry humor. Avoid generic dashboards, floating text, and unnecessary cutaways.
- Keep captions verbatim to final audio; design captions after voice timing is locked.
- Use fillers only when they reveal character. Never scatter them mechanically.
- For an authorized real person, preserve recognizable cadence without inventing harmful factual claims.

## Quality Gates

- Beginning, middle, and end are identifiable in one sentence each.
- The final line is the strongest comic beat and closes the premise.
- Every sentence either advances mechanism, escalation, or payoff.
- Character voices remain distinguishable without speaker labels.
- Facts, roadmap, and satire remain legible to the intended audience.
- Spoken duration fits the target at the intended delivery pace, including pauses.
- Visual direction contains actions and reactions, not a slide deck.
- No generic AI slogans, empty superlatives, synthetic rhetorical trios, or over-explained joke.

## Deliverable

Return:

1. A one-line premise and comic engine.
2. The final dialogue.
3. A timed beat sheet with camera, action, expression, and transition.
4. Voice and sound direction.
5. Duration evidence and the claim labels used.
